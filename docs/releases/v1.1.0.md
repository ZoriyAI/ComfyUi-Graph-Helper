# NodeWise Studio 1.1.0

Lessons now ask you things, not only the canvas: checklists, rubrics, questions and checks the app makes, each saying
whether the app saw it or you said it. Lessons have levels, tracks and a path; 95 lessons in sixteen groups, with the
new groups Start and a reliable setup, Experiments and quality, Diagnostics, performance and safety, and The daily
work cycle. Lesson progress is saved again after the 3D lesson or one of My lessons (1.0.0 lost
it). A model of Ollama's cloud is no longer called local. A full guide to choosing a model, with current suggestions
and prices. Copyright and authorship are stated in NOTICE and COPYRIGHT, and the packages carry them.

## Update from 1.0.0 if you use lessons

Version 1.0.0 had a defect: once you opened or read the lesson "A picture into a 3D model: Hunyuan3D 2" (its id is
`3d-image`) or any lesson from My lessons, the app stopped saving lesson progress. Every later save was refused, in
every lesson, until the app was closed: the steps you read, the lessons you finished and the lesson that was open.
The window still showed them, so nothing looked wrong.

What this means for you:

- Progress saved before that moment is kept, and 1.1.0 reads it as it is.
- Progress made after it, in the same run of the app, was never written. 1.1.0 cannot bring it back: open those
  lessons again (a doing step that is already on the canvas ticks itself off at once).
- After a restart 1.0.0 saved again, until the next time one of those lessons was opened.
- The 1.0.0 notes say that a lesson open from My lessons comes back after a restart. In 1.0.0 it did not, for the
  same reason. In 1.1.0 it does.

Update to 1.1.0: the fix is there, and nothing else is needed. Your settings, projects and lessons stay.

## Lessons

- **Steps you answer.** Besides the steps to read and the steps on the canvas, a lesson can have a checklist of things
  to do outside the app, a rubric (a question per criterion with two to four levels), questions to check yourself,
  and a check the app makes from what it already reads (ComfyUI was read, a node or a model file is there, every node
  of the workflow is installed). An item the app sees says **The app sees this**; your tick says **Your own word**.
  When the app cannot tell, **I checked it myself** lets you go on. **Clear my answers** takes your answers back.
- **Your own task:** a step without a ready answer and without **Do this for me**; what is still missing stays folded
  until you ask for it.
- **What a lesson ends with**, in three states: **Structure checked** (the app checked the graph on the canvas),
  **Run in ComfyUI** and **Result assessed**. Each says who stands behind it: checked by the app, your own word, or
  not yet. The app runs nothing and does not see your pictures.
- **Levels, tracks and the path.** Every lesson has a level from 0 (setting up ComfyUI) to 5 (projects), a place
  (core, professional or in depth) and its tracks. **Show the lessons** reads the list by **Topics**, **Levels**,
  **Tracks** or as a **Path**: the recommended order for one track, numbered, with what is done marked. Every lesson
  stays open.
- **About this lesson** on lessons of the new standard: the skill, what you need before, the versions it was written
  for and the date it was checked, common mistakes, what the app does not check, and the source.
- 95 lessons in sixteen groups: Start and a reliable setup, ComfyUI basics, Pictures: first steps, Video,
  WanVideoWrapper, Pictures: models, Video: the anchor picture, Packs and tools, Pictures: techniques, Sound and
  speech, 3D, Faces and characters, Experiments and quality, Diagnostics, performance and safety, The daily work
  cycle, and For adults (18+).
- **My lessons:** **Add a self-check** adds a checklist step, one thing to do per line. A lesson imported or drafted
  with a rubric, questions or a check the app makes keeps those steps; the editor lets you change their titles and
  words.
- **Lesson files, version 2.** A lesson file can hold the new steps, what the lesson ends with and the lesson's level,
  tracks and template. A file that uses none of it is still written as version 1, so 1.0.0 opens it; 1.1.0 reads both.
  [The lesson file format](../wiki/lessons/Lesson-File-Format.md)
- For people who write lessons: `node scripts/lesson-lint.js` checks a lesson against the app's rules and the new
  template and says in plain words what is missing. [Writing a lesson](../wiki/dev/Writing-A-Lesson.md)

New lessons, in the new group Start and a reliable setup, the first in the list. The app changes nothing in
ComfyUI: installing, updating and the Manager are steps you tick, and the app checks what it can read.

- **Desktop, Portable, a manual install or Cloud.** A written choice of how you run ComfyUI, with its limits, made
  before anything is installed. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Choose-Setup.md)
- **Install and a first run without errors.** A working install proven by a first picture, and a card of your
  setup: system, graphics card, version, way of install and folders. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/First-Run.md)
- **A model library and shared folders.** Models in the folders ComfyUI looks in, one copy of each, and a list of
  the important files with their hashes. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Model-Library.md)
- **The Manager: a first node pack, safely.** A first pack installed through the Manager, its source and version
  checked, the import log clean, and a run that uses its node. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/First-Pack.md)
- **Snapshots, updates and the way back.** An update made with a tested point to return to, a control workflow run
  before and after, and a written way back. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Snapshots.md)

New lessons, in the group Experiments and quality:

- **A controlled experiment: one change at a time.** Change exactly one setting between two runs, say what it did,
  and confirm it with a repeat. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/One-Factor.md)
- **A quality rubric and a blind comparison.** Choose between two variants by criteria written in advance, scored
  without knowing which is which. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Blind-Compare.md)
- **Model card: family, parts and license.** A card for one model with its sources and what is unknown, and the
  model tried with a LoRA of its own family. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Reading-A-Model-Card.md)

New lessons, in the new group Diagnostics, performance and safety:

- **Diagnosing missing nodes, models and IMPORT FAILED.** One failure named with evidence, a small graph known to
  run, and a fix confirmed by a second run. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Diagnose-Missing.md)
- **Auditing someone else's workflow and a custom pack.** A written verdict on a workflow and its packs: run, run
  with limits, or do not run, with the evidence and the unknowns. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Audit-Workflow.md)
- **Partner nodes: data, licenses and the cost of a run.** What a workflow sends out and under which terms, the cost
  of a series, and a local way that sends nothing. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Partner-Cost.md)

New lessons, in the new group The daily work cycle:

- **Templates: from a ready workflow to your own.** Your own version of a template, saved apart from it, with its
  inputs, outputs, models and notes written down. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Template-Own.md)
- **Run, queue, history and cancel.** Several runs queued on purpose, one cancelled while it waited, one stopped while
  it ran, and a finished one found in the history. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Queue-History.md)
- **Partial execution and the cache.** A cheap preview branch beside the saver, and proof of which branch ran and why
  a run was faster. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Partial-Cache.md)
- **Logs and a minimal failing example.** A failure read in the log, a workflow cut down to the few nodes that still
  fail, and a report another person can repeat. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Minimal-Example.md)
- **JSON, API JSON and the workflow inside a PNG.** One graph as a workflow, as an API file and as a picture that
  carries it, each opened again and checked for private values. [The lesson](../wiki/lessons/Workflow-Files.md)

## The assistant

- **Ollama's cloud models are named as leaving the computer.** Ollama serves some models from its own cloud through
  the Ollama on your computer, under names that end in `:cloud` or `-cloud` (for example `gemma4:cloud` or
  `gpt-oss:120b-cloud`). With such a model on **Your own server**, 1.0.0 still said that nothing leaves the computer,
  which was not true. Now the model list marks it **Ollama's cloud**, and Settings ▸ Assistant, the assistant chip in
  the top bar, Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ **What is sent to the model** and the conversation log say that this model runs
  in Ollama's cloud and that your question and the graph leave this computer through Ollama. The request still goes
  to your Ollama's address. There is no switch to call such a model local.
  [Ollama](../wiki/assistant/Ollama.md#ollamas-cloud-models)
- Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ **What is sent to the model** now also says where a question goes now: your own server, the
  cloud provider you chose, or Ollama's cloud.
- **Which model to take** is a full guide: models by hardware with download sizes and estimated memory, Ollama's
  cloud and what leaves the computer then, each provider's suggestions with prices as of 15 November 2026,
  OpenAI-compatible services, what size is enough for asking, building and drafting lessons, and a two-minute test.
  The models were not tried on real hardware, and the guide says so.
  [Which model to take](../wiki/assistant/Which-Model.md)
- For your own server the app now suggests `qwen3.5:9b` (about 8 GB of video memory), `gpt-oss:20b` (about 16 GB)
  and `qwen3.6:27b` (about 24 GB), all marked "tools" in Ollama's library. The memory figures are estimates.
- Prices of the older cloud models the providers still list (OpenAI `gpt-4o` and `gpt-4o-mini`, Anthropic Opus 4 and
  4.1, more Google Gemini models), and the context windows of `qwen3.5`, `qwen3.6`, `qwen3.8` and `gemma4`, are known
  to the app, so the cost of a turn and the window are shown for them.

## Windows and macOS: install scripts

There are still no packages for Windows and macOS. The release carries `install-windows.ps1` (Windows 10 22H2 and 11,
x64) and `install-macos.sh` (macOS 13 or newer, Apple silicon and Intel), listed in `SHA256SUMS`. They build the app
from this release's source and install it for your user, as in 1.0.0. The app is not signed by a publisher.

**Checked on GitHub's machines, not yet on real computers.** Every change now runs both scripts on GitHub's Windows
machines (Windows Server 2022 and 2025, with Windows PowerShell 5.1 and PowerShell 7) and macOS machines (Apple
silicon with macOS 26 and Intel with macOS 15): they build, install, start, upgrade and remove the app there. They have
not yet been tried on a range of real computers. If you try one, please report what happened in the project's Issues
(there is a form for it), with the log file the script names at the end (`%TEMP%\nodewise-studio-install.log` on
Windows, `~/Library/Logs/nodewise-studio-install.log` on macOS) and your system version. The manual build pages remain
the fallback.
[Installing on Windows](../wiki/start/Installing-On-Windows.md), [Installing on macOS](../wiki/start/Installing-On-macOS.md)

## What the checks do not prove

A clean graph check means that the static checks of the catalog read, the graph's structure, inputs and outputs,
values, model files and known families found no problem. It is not a run in ComfyUI and no promise of quality or of
enough graphics memory. An incomplete or outdated catalog, and metadata the app does not know, remain limits. A
passed lesson step means the canvas holds what the step asks for, not that the picture will be good. A ticked
checklist item, a rubric score and **I checked it myself** are your own word: the app ran nothing in ComfyUI and did
not see your pictures. A check the app makes reads only what the app already knows.
